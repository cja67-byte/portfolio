Project story / Literature-based research

When materials meet the human body.

Biocompatibility in prosthetics and implants.
ENG 102 · New Jersey Institute of Technology · April 1, 2025

Download the paper (Word) ↓
01 /

The question

What makes a material compatible with the body?

My paper explores the challenges biocompatibility poses for prosthetics and implants, and the approaches published literature discusses for addressing them. The focus is on immune response, material safety, and patient-specific design.

02 /

What I did

Connecting ideas across published research.

I brought together sources on immune response, cytotoxicity, and customization, organizing them into a literature-based argument. The paper discusses surface coatings, material selection, 3D printing, testing, affordability, and ethical considerations. This project demonstrates research writing, comparing technical approaches, and connecting engineering questions to patient needs.

03 /

Key themes

Materials. Customization. Access.

The paper considers material selection and coatings alongside the body’s response. It discusses customization and 3D printing in relation to patient-specific design, and considers affordability and ethical questions alongside technical approaches. These are themes from the literature, not findings from an experiment I conducted.

04 /

My direction

From prosthetics to biomaterials development.

This topic connects my interest in prosthetics with my goal of working in biomaterials development. In the paper, I discuss my interest in biocompatible coatings and more affordable, personalized prosthetics.

05 /

Project scope

Research writing, not a tested device.

This is a literature-based class paper, not an experimental study, clinical recommendation, or tested implant design. The original paper contains the full discussion and reference list. Original title: Biocompatibility: A Mechanical Hurdle in Prosthetics and Implants.
